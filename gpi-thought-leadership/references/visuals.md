# Visual identification, creation, and inspection

## Preserve useful coverage

Use the selected brand's targets. The inspected GPI and inBeat profiles aim for one inline visual per 700 words, rounded, with a floor of two and cap of eight, plus two sourced examples. Count delivered assets separately from planned or selected assets. Recreated charts/illustrations do not count as externally sourced image captures.

Create roughly twice the target as concise candidates, reusing research discoveries. Track one ID per candidate: `V` for original charts/diagrams, `SRC` for observed source images, `ILL` for illustrations. Record reader question, visible information, understanding lost if removed, evidence IDs, composition, destination section and exact adjacent paragraph. Each ID ends in selected, retained, rejected, or pending with a reason; never generate duplicate IDs in different buckets.

Admission requires all three:

1. Shows useful information or a relationship that prose alone communicates less well.
2. Has one clear idea a skimmer can learn independently.
3. Changes understanding of a decision, mechanism, example, or comparison in this article.

Reject quote/definition cards, paragraphs in boxes, generic topic metaphors, decorative inline stock images, and two-value charts that merely repeat a sentence. Captions cannot supply information absent from the image.

## Find real images

Actively search one to three artifact-specific opportunities from existing article sections. An existing chart/video does not waive discovery. A real artifact may fulfill a need initially imagined as a custom diagram.

Reuse captures already obtained during research. While useful source/overall coverage is short, try distinct strategies, up to five: web artifact search, original research reports, PDF reports, official newsrooms, and interface walkthroughs. Stop when targets are met. Cache URL captures and query+market+strategy results. Search only for unmet needs on later rounds.

Keep each query/strategy, visited page, observed image ID, source URL, and selection/rejection reason. Prefer the original publisher and avoid this publication's own domains when sourcing external examples. Reject logos, avatars, navigation, generic headers, and ordinary text screenshots.

Observe the actual image on its public page or PDF before selection. Bind the choice to its observed image ID, exact page URL, and original file URL; do not invent asset URLs from snippets. Inspect the labeled candidate pixels. Record source identity, capture time, original URL, adjacent context, file hash, and reuse basis. Attribution alone is not a reuse license. Respect tool restrictions on downloads and display; use permitted capture/acquisition routes.

Preserve complete axes, legends, labels, and material limitations. Keep original source colors. Use the identical inspected bytes when placing the image rather than recapturing a changed responsive element. Place it directly after the paragraph explaining its visible detail, with a linked source credit and useful caption. Avoid irrelevant or paragraph-only screenshots; an exact-text artifact needs a specific reason its wording is itself the subject of analysis.

## Create original visuals

Pick the form that expresses the relationship:

| Relationship | Suitable form | Integrity requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparable magnitudes | Bar or dot plot | Verified values; common units/populations/periods; truthful scale |
| Measured change over time | Line chart | Actual time spacing and values from evidence |
| Sequence | Numbered spine/flow | Clear order; long sequences flow vertically |
| Conditional choice | Decision tree | One root, explicit conditions, reachable outcomes |
| Tradeoffs | Comparison matrix | Comparable dimensions and supported entries |
| Mechanism | Structural diagram or conceptual illustration | Specific relationships; labeled conceptual if not measured |
| Specific inaccessible artifact | Labeled recreation | Cited evidence supports all labels/values; visibly described as recreated |

Use deterministic plotting or SVG/HTML for factual charts and precise diagrams. Retain editable sources and PNG exports at least 1600px wide for original graphics. Use the brand's actual font and tokens, clean space, direct labels, and at most one focal accent. Do not shrink text or hide labels to make an overcrowded diagram pass; simplify or split it.

Use an available image-generation tool for at most two admitted illustrations by default. Read its skill/instructions when applicable. A generation request names composition, objects/relationships, brand tokens, prohibited inventions, evidence IDs, exact placement, alt text and caption. Generate one candidate per admitted request initially. Conceptual images need a concrete mechanism, not rockets/handshakes/funnels as topic metaphors. Artifact recreation must not fabricate a campaign, logo, interface, metric, or outcome beyond its evidence.

Charts carry titles, values, units, legends, and essential annotations inside the image. Place explanatory prose and source credits in one accessible caption beneath it. Keep a short "illustrative / not measured data" caveat inside conceptual quantitative-looking graphics so they remain truthful when opened alone.

Hero photography is a separate optional brand/destination decision. The engine's nature-photo hero preference does not justify decorative inline filler or count toward explanatory source coverage.

## Inspect pixels, then inspect the article

For each actual file, inspect at full size and at article width. An image-capable reviewer receives labeled pixels plus relevant source excerpts, caption, and adjacent paragraph. Record findings for:

- legibility and clipping;
- evidence/geometry accuracy;
- explanatory value;
- visual specificity;
- removal loss (what understanding disappears without it);
- brand fidelity for original graphics;
- source identity, placement, and linked credit for sourced images.

Then review the set for repeated content/composition and sections over 900 words with no useful visual. Review the final article layout. A set-level review must not reopen unchanged individual usefulness decisions under a new name.

Reuse a complete passing per-image review only when pixels, metadata, evidence, adjacent section, and visual policy are unchanged. Fix placement/caption defects without reacquiring/regenerating; recheck the changed caption's accuracy or placement against the existing pixels and evidence before renewing approval. Preserve editor uploads on ordinary revisions; surface a failing upload rather than silently dropping or replacing it.

## Recovery and honest completion

Failed acquisition excludes that asset, not the reader need. Retain successful assets and try another source. A selected-but-unavailable image remains a visible gap until replaced, fulfilled, or explicitly dispositioned. Required explanations get one alternative before reporting the unresolved need.

Do not repeatedly regenerate an optional image to force acceptance. Allow one focused repair for a diagnosed defect when useful and affordable. If an illustration remains weak, omit it with a reason; do not claim a pending brief was visually inspected.

For a shortfall, record target, selected, delivered, sourced, rounds, every search tried, long sections lacking visuals, and status `met`, `below_target`, or `exception`. Fewer images can be valid after the distinct searches are exhausted or acquisition/budget is blocked and documented. "Tried once and cut everything" is not adequate effort. Report a budget-caused shortfall as such, not as proof that no suitable images exist.

An unresolved asset request specifies ID, type, what it shows, why it helps, exact source/screen, blocker/fallback, caption, source line, alt text and placement. Such requests remain visible and prevent a publish-ready claim if the article depends on them. Optional video is separate: verify relevance and availability, retain a watch link, and never infer content from an unwatched/unread source.
